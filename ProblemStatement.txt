# Problem Statement: SIP Insight Lab — AI-Powered Discovery Engine

As a Product Manager on the Growth team at Groww, my goal is to increase the
percentage of first-time mutual fund investors who still have an active SIP 12
months after starting their first one. An active SIP means at least one
successful instalment in the last 30 days.

First-time investors stop, pause or lose their SIPs for very different reasons.
Some SIPs end without any decision at all: a debit fails because the salary
hasn't arrived yet, and after three misses the SIP is cancelled. Others stop
because the app or payment flow confused them, because early returns missed
what a calculator, friend or finfluencer promised, because their income
changed, or because they made a deliberate, informed choice. Each of these
needs a different fix, yet the evidence is scattered across app reviews,
Reddit, investing forums and YouTube comments, where no one can tell them apart
or measure how often each happens.

Three traps make this evidence easy to misread. First, saying is not doing: a
user who writes "I'm thinking of stopping" or sounds negative has not
necessarily stopped, so intent and action must be recorded separately. Second,
not every stop is a decision: an involuntary lapse and a chosen stop look
identical in a retention metric but call for opposite solutions. Third,
studying only the people who leave hides half the answer: what protects the
investors who stay matters as much as what pushes others out.

SIP Insight Lab is an AI-powered discovery engine that addresses this. It
collects permitted public feedback from Google Play, the App Store, Reddit,
TradingQ&A and YouTube, removes personal details, and classifies each item
against a consistent research taxonomy. For each item it records the investor's
lifecycle stage and what they actually did, and keeps that separate from what
they said they would do. It also records the kind of stop, the root cause, the
moment that triggered the change, where they went for help outside the app,
and, for those who continued, what kept them going.

From this, the engine reveals:
- the gap between stated intent and actual action
- how much churn comes from silent, involuntary lapses
- when in the first 12 months doubt or failure strikes
- what separates continuers from those who drop off
- how these patterns differ by age, income stability, source of money and who
  influenced the investor

Every insight links back to its original quotes and sources. A second AI agent
challenges each leading theme by searching for counter-evidence. The strongest
opportunities become hypothesis cards, each paired with an interview guide for
validation.

Quality is measured, not assumed. The engine's classifications are compared
against a hand-labelled golden set of at least 20 examples. Agreement and
errors are reported openly, low-confidence items go to human review, and every
correction or rejection of AI output is logged in an exportable AI Evidence
Log. Opportunities are ranked using a visible formula based on frequency,
severity, concentration in the first six months, and whether the problem can be
solved without investment advice or fear-based nudges.

Public feedback is treated as a source of hypotheses, not as a measure of
retention or proof of causality. Findings will be validated through user
interviews and, where available, internal behavioural data. The solution will
never recommend specific funds or stocks, and will not rely on fear, FOMO,
urgency or manipulative nudges.

The goal is to replace scattered opinions with traceable evidence. That
evidence should show Groww not just why first-time investors stop, but which
stops a better product could have prevented.
